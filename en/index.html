GTU Electronics Engineering Lecture Notes

Lecture notes, past exams, homework, lab reports and projects from all 7 semesters of the Electronics Engineering undergraduate programme at Gebze Technical University (GTU, Turkey), shared for free. Most course pages are in Turkish with English course names and summaries; many handwritten notes also have searchable digital (LaTeX) versions.

    Semesters and courses

    Semester 1 · Fall 2021–22

    • Atatürk's Principles and History of the Turkish Revolution IThe late Ottoman period, the Turkish War of Independence and the founding of the Republic of Türkiye.
    • Algorithms and Programming I INF211Introduction to algorithms and programming in Python: data types, functions, lists and file handling; weekly lab assignments and three projects.
    • Introduction to Electrical Engineering ELM101An introduction to the sub-fields of electrical and electronics engineering, with weekly overview lectures on topics such as electronics, signal processing and cryptology.
    • Physics I FİZ121Mechanics: vectors, kinematics, Newton's laws, work and energy, momentum and rotational motion.
    • Physics I LaboratoryMechanics experiments: lab reports and final practice questions.
    • Occupational Health and Safety IHow workplace accidents happen, working at height, manual handling and construction safety; occupational health and safety in Türkiye and worldwide.
    • Linear AlgebraMatrices and determinants, systems of linear equations, vector spaces, linear transformations, eigenvalues and eigenvectors.
    • Mathematics I MAT101Limits, continuity, derivatives and integrals of single-variable functions and their applications.

    Semester 2 · Spring 2021–22

    • Atatürk's Principles and History of the Turkish Revolution IIPolitical, legal and social reforms of the Republican era; Atatürk's principles.
    • Algorithms and Programming II INF212Data structures and algorithms in Python: binary search and AVL trees, hashing and graphs; lab assignments and two projects.
    • Scientific MethodologyKnowledge and science, the scientific method (facts, hypotheses, laws, theories) and the history of science.
    • Physics IIElectricity and magnetism: electric fields, Gauss's law, electric potential, capacitance, current and resistance, magnetic fields and induction.
    • Occupational Health and Safety IIOccupational health and safety legislation, risk assessment and protective measures.
    • Materials Science MBM104Atomic structure and bonding, crystal structures and defects, mechanical and electrical properties of materials.
    • Mathematics IISequences and series, functions of several variables, partial derivatives and multiple integrals.

    Semester 3 · Fall 2022–23

    • Circuit Theory IBasic circuit analysis: Kirchhoff's laws, nodal and mesh analysis, Thevenin/Norton equivalents, first- and second-order circuits.
    • Circuit Theory I Laboratory ELM226Circuit Theory I experiments: pre-lab work, Multisim simulations, measurements and the lab final.
    • Differential Equations MAT215First-order equations, higher-order linear equations, the Cauchy–Euler equation, reduction of order, series solutions and the Laplace transform.
    • Electronic Devices ELM231Semiconductor physics, the pn junction and diodes, and the structure, operation and biasing of BJTs and MOSFETs.
    • EnglishEnglish course: course materials and a vocabulary list for the midterm.
    • Numerical Analysis MAT214Root finding, interpolation, numerical differentiation and integration, direct and iterative methods for linear systems, and numerical solution of ODEs.
    • Turkish I TÜR101Turkish language: morphology, syntax and written-expression exercises.

    Semester 4 · Spring 2022–23

    • Circuit Theory II ELM227Sinusoidal steady state and AC power, circuit analysis with the Laplace transform, frequency response and passive filters, Fourier series.
    • Circuit Theory II Laboratory ELM228Circuit Theory II experiments: lab sheets, pre-lab work, MATLAB calculations and Multisim simulations.
    • Electronics ISignals and amplifiers; BJT and MOSFET amplifiers: DC biasing, small-signal models, common-emitter, common-base and common-collector stages.
    • Electronics Laboratory I ELEC237Electronics I experiments, LTspice simulations and a lab final with µA741 op-amp circuits (adder, subtractor, integrator).
    • Signals and Systems ELM264Continuous- and discrete-time signals, LTI systems and convolution, Fourier series and transforms, Laplace and z-transforms.
    • Logic Circuits and Design ELM234 / ELM235Number systems, Boolean algebra and logic gates, combinational and sequential circuits, finite-state machines and ALU design; SystemVerilog lab work.
    • Probability and Randomness ELM218Probability axioms, conditional probability and Bayes' rule, random variables and distributions, moment-generating functions, limit theorems and random processes.
    • Turkish IITurkish II: written expression and composition assignments.

    Semester 5 · Fall 2023–24

    • Analog Communication Systems ELM361Fourier analysis, amplitude modulation (AM, DSB, SSB), angle modulation (FM, PM), receivers and noise in communication systems.
    • Electromagnetic Field Theory ELM341Vector analysis, electrostatics (Coulomb's and Gauss's laws, potential, boundary conditions), magnetostatics and Maxwell's equations.
    • Electromechanical Energy ConversionMagnetic circuits, transformers, principles of electromechanical energy conversion, DC and AC machines.
    • Electronics IIMultistage and differential amplifiers, current mirrors, frequency response, feedback and output stages.
    • Electronics Laboratory II ELEC337Low- and high-frequency behaviour of single-BJT amplifiers, LTspice simulations, the lab final and a differential-amplifier project.
    • Microprocessors ELEC334ARM Cortex-M0+ architecture and the STM32G0 microcontroller: programming in assembly and C, memory and peripherals, interrupts and timers.
    • Microprocessors Laboratory ELEC335Assembly and C experiments on the STM32G0 Nucleo board (e.g. GPIO, UART) and lab reports.

    Semester 6 · Spring 2023–24

    • Electromagnetic Wave TheoryTime-harmonic fields and plane waves, lossy media, reflection and refraction (Brewster angle) and waveguides.
    • Communications Laboratory ELM366Experiments on amplitude modulation (DSB-LC/SC), SSB and FM, and ASK modulation: pre-lab work and reports.
    • Control Systems ELEC322Mathematical modelling (transfer functions, state space), transient and steady-state analysis, stability, root locus, PID and compensator design.
    • Digital Communications ELM365Sampling and pulse-code modulation, baseband transmission, optimum receivers, digital modulation (ASK, PSK, FSK) and bit-error probability.
    • Fundamentals of Digital Signal Processing ELM367Discrete-time signals and systems, convolution, the z-transform, the discrete Fourier transform and FFT, and digital filter structures.
    • Digital Signal Processing Laboratory ELM368Weekly DSP exercises in Python/Jupyter (pre-lab work and code) and a walking-vs-running classification project.

    Semester 7 · Fall 2024–25

    • Image Processing ELM463Digital image fundamentals, point operations and histograms, spatial filtering, morphological operations and segmentation; Python labs and a traffic-sign detection project.
    • RF System Design for Communications ELM667RF transceiver systems: gain, noise, nonlinearity (P1dB, IP3) and system budgets; a 4G LTE term project.
    • Fundamentals of Machine Learning ELM472Supervised and unsupervised learning, regression and classification, Bayesian decision theory, dimensionality reduction, clustering and anomaly detection, and an introduction to neural networks.

    FAQ

    Where can I find GTU Electronics Engineering lecture notes?

    Pick a course above. Every course page lists lecture notes, recitation notes, slides, past exams, homework, lab sheets and projects with direct links to the files in the GitHub repository.

    Are there past exams?

    Yes, many courses include past midterms, finals and quizzes. They belong to the instructors and GTU and are shared for educational purposes only.

    Can I contribute?

    Yes: open an issue or a pull request on GitHub.
